## Statement and evidence status

## 1. The base ring and the equation $N^2=7t$

Start with the cyclotomic base $\mathbf Q(\tau)$, where $\tau$ has order 12. The prime 7 is unramified in this field. Choose a complete strictly Henselian discrete valuation ring (DVR) $R$ above 7, with residue field $k$ algebraically closed, fraction field $K$, and

$$
v_R(7)=1.
$$

Do not adjoin $\sqrt7$ before choosing this base: after that ramified extension the valuation of 7 is 2, and the irreducibility that forces the tube rank would be lost.

For a rank-one matrix $M=ab^T$, Weyl expansion and trace orthogonality give

$$
\sum_p Z_pZ_{-p}=6\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)=6N^2.
$$

On the equal-overlap scheme the zero-label term is $N^2$, while the 35 nonzero terms equal $t$. Therefore $N^2+35t=6N^2$, and

$$
N^2=7t.\tag{1}
$$

This is an identity over the 7-adic DVR itself. It does not require a square root of 7.

## 2. No horizontal branch reaches the boundary

Let $C$ be the schematic closure in $X_R=\mathbf P^5_R\times\mathbf P^5_R$ of the entire generic open scheme $U_{6,K}$, retaining its scheme structure. Saturation by the uniformizer removes uniformizer torsion, so $C$ is flat over $R$. We prove

$$
C_k\cap V(t)=\varnothing.\tag{2}
$$

If (2) failed, the finite-type valuative criterion would give a DVR trait with generic point in $t\ne0$ and special point in $t=0$. Since 6 is a unit at 7, Weyl expansion is integral and invertible after separately making $a,b$ primitive. The resulting weights are nonnegative and have minimum zero. For each nonzero label they satisfy

$$
w_p+w_{-p}=\mu>0,
\qquad 2w_0=v(7)+\mu.
$$

The trait's zero-weight support is disjoint from its negative, has size at least 6, and its residue matrix satisfies the rank-one and initial equal-overlap equations. The weight-cell proof allows unbounded $\mu$, arbitrary rational denominators, and residue-field extensions. Its solver normalization rescales valuation coordinates only. The local DVR used in the quadratic argument above still has $v_R(7)=1$.

The complete support audit gives 112 realizable supports in six $SL_2$-types. Every type is reduced to its listed representative by the exact integral symmetries, with the residual point or component normalization proved in the accompanying certificate. Across the six families, [e40_replay_boundary.py](../sources/d6/numerics/e40_replay_boundary.py) replays 147 exact unit identities and constructs six fresh rational-linear weight solvers. The output [e40_replay_boundary.json](../sources/d6/numerics/e40_replay_boundary.json) records `identity_count=147`, `complete_boundary_coverage=true`, and six cover records with `status="unsat"`, `uses_e31=false`, and `uses_saved_smt=false`. A unit identity for the initial ideal of a cell contradicts the residue point required by the trait. Thus no such trait exists, proving (2).

This argument covers every point of the generic scheme, including a hypothetical free orbit. It does not infer generic finiteness from a modular orbit count.

## 3. Finite flat closure and the special-fibre lengths

The complete open special fibre at 7 has 3456 geometric points. Each local ring is $k[N]/(N^2)$, so its total scheme length is 6912. The inherited exact input [e13_complete_d6.json](../sources/special-fibres/numerics/e13_complete_d6.json) records `complete_fibre=true`, `trace_zero_chart_length=3456`, `distinct_chart_points=3456`, `whole_fibre_points=3456`, `whole_fibre_length=6912`, and `trace_section_reduced=true`.

The chart shifts cover the whole open fibre because $t\ne0$ forces $Z_{(0,1)}=\sum_i\omega^ia_ib_i\ne0$, so some $a_ib_i\ne0$. Applying $X^{-i}$ moves that index to zero in both vectors. Separate projective rescaling then puts the point in $a_0=b_0=1$. Thus the $X$-translates of this chart cover the open fibre. The dimension-six record gives this coverage in its `proof` field and counts the union in `whole_fibre_points`. The corresponding dimension-five coverage field is named `whole_fibre_coverage`.

The replay stage also verifies all-point Jacobian ranks $(9,10)$, the local dual-number algebra, and the special group action. The full replay recomputes the trace-section calculation and requires dimension zero and length 3456. A saved transcript check alone does not rerun this upper bound.

By (2), $C_k$ is a closed subscheme of this finite open special fibre, hence is finite. Properness and upper semicontinuity of fibre dimension then imply that $C$ is finite. Since it is flat over the DVR, it is finite flat. At this point its generic rank is bounded by the special-fibre length, but equality still requires showing that all local tubes occur and that each has rank two.

The exact 6a seed in [e01_6a.gp](../sources/d6-seed/numerics/e01_6a.gp) and [e20_hermitian_6a.gp](../sources/d4-upper/numerics/e20_hermitian_6a.gp) supplies one nonescaping point: exact unit polynomials cover all 35 nonzero labels, so its reduction lies in $t\ne0$. The special extended Clifford group acts transitively on the 3456 special points. Its integral action carries the seed's tube to every point. Thus every local tube is nonempty.

After the strictly Henselian base change, decompose the finite algebra of $C$ into local factors $A_x$, one for each special point $x$. The special local algebra is $k[N]/(N^2)$, so

$$
0<\operatorname{rank}_R(A_x)
 =\dim_k(A_x/7A_x)\le2.\tag{3}
$$

The strict inequality is the nonempty-tube conclusion above. Trivialize $\mathcal O(1,1)$ on each local finite algebra $A_x$ and use the induced trivialization of $\mathcal O(2,2)$. The sections $N$ and $t$ then become functions satisfying $N^2=7t$, with $t$ a unit. Because the residue field is algebraically closed and 2 is invertible, a residue square root of $t$ lifts by Hensel's lemma to a unit $u\in A_x$ with $u^2=t$. Equation (1) gives

$$
\left(N/u\right)^2=7.
$$

The polynomial $T^2-7$ is Eisenstein over $R$, since $v_R(7)=1$, so $K(\sqrt7)$ is a separable quadratic field over $K$. Sending $\sqrt7$ to $N/u$ gives a unital map

$$
K(\sqrt7)\longrightarrow (A_x)_K.
$$

The target may initially have nilpotents, but a unital homomorphism from a field into a nonzero algebra is injective. Hence $\dim_K(A_x)_K$ is a positive even integer. By (3) it is at most two, so it equals two and the map is an isomorphism. Each tube is therefore the separable field $K(\sqrt7)$, with exactly two distinct geometric generic points and no generic nilpotents.

Summing over the 3456 special points gives

$$
\operatorname{length}(U_{6,\overline K})=3456\cdot2=6912,
$$

and the generic scheme is reduced. The no-escape result identifies the generic fibre of $C$ with the original open scheme, so this is the desired complex count. The special-fibre count alone would give no such conclusion. Finite flatness, nonempty tubes, and the quadratic-field injection supply the generic argument.

## 4. Full stabilizers and orbit counts

The exact special-fibre group certificate gives an ordinary Clifford group of order 5184, an algebraic factor-swap extension of order 10368, a transitive extended action on the special support, and stabilizer $C_3$ at every special point. The nonidentity stabilizer elements have actual symplectic trace $5=-1\pmod6$. At 7 the group action extends integrally and its projective reduction is faithful.

Fix a special point and its two generic points in the tube. The special $C_3$ acts on those two points, giving a homomorphism $C_3\to S_2$. This homomorphism is trivial, so the full special $C_3$ fixes each generic point. Conversely, any generic stabilizer element specializes to an element fixing the special point and therefore lies in that same $C_3$. The complete generic stabilizer is exactly $C_3$, contained in the ordinary Clifford subgroup. Its actual symplectic trace remains 5.

Orbit–stabilizer gives the classification:

| Action | Group order | Orbit decomposition |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary projective Clifford | 5184 | $4\times1728$ |
| Algebraic factor-swap extension | 10368 | $2\times3456$ |
| Weyl–Heisenberg displacements | 36 | $192\times36$ |

An exact order-two Galois automorphism preserving $\tau$ sends the Hermitian 6a seed to a non-Hermitian seed, so these give the two disjoint extended orbits. The Weyl–Heisenberg displacement action is free: a nontrivial displacement fixing a trace-one projector would force the overlap product to equal both 1 and $1/7$. It follows that there are $6912/36=192$ displacement orbits.

Here the extension acts algebraically by transpose/factor swap. On non-Hermitian points it is not described as a physical antiunitary action.
