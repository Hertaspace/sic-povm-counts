# Dimension seven: finiteness, the exact count, and reducedness

**Author:** Yaoling Yang

## Statement and conventions

Let

$$
 X_7=\mathbb P^6_{\mathbb C}\times\mathbb P^6_{\mathbb C},\qquad
 S=b^Ta,
$$

and let $D_p$, $p=(p_1,p_2)\in\mathbb F_7^2$, be the Weyl displacements in the convention

$$
 \tau=-e^{\pi i/7}=\zeta^4,\quad \zeta=e^{2\pi i/7},\quad
 D_{(i,j)}=\tau^{ij}X^iZ^j.
$$

Labels are formed as signed integers before reduction modulo 7. For the 24 unordered pairs $\{p,-p\}$ with $p\ne0$, put

$$
 q_p(a,b)=(b^TD_pa)(b^TD_{-p}a)-S^2/8.
$$

Each $q_p$ is bihomogeneous of bidegree $(2,2)$. The open solution scheme $Y_7$ is defined by all these equations on $S\ne0$. It counts projectors $\Pi=ab^T/S$, without a Hermiticity condition or a symmetry quotient. The 24 displayed forms span a 15-dimensional space. See [ACFW, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05981) for the displacement convention.

The result is

$$
 \boxed{Y_7\text{ is finite, reduced, and has }32928\text{ complex points}.}
$$

This is an exact computer-assisted proof combined with the local intersection argument below. It is not a formal proof-assistant development. The proof has four independent inputs: finiteness, a boundary-path lower bound, an off-base lower bound, and an exact lower bound from algebraic solutions. None of the first three is inferred from search completion.

## 1. Finiteness is established before the intersection budget

### Boundary supports at the prime 29

Use the Weyl-coordinate compactification in the finiteness certificates. Write its coordinates as $z_p$, with boundary $z_0=0$, and use the certificate normalization in which the open equations include

$$
 z_pz_{-p}=t,\qquad z_0^2=8t.
$$

At the boundary, the geometric support

$$
 \Sigma=\{p:z_p\ne0\}
$$

satisfies $0\notin\Sigma$, $\Sigma\cap(-\Sigma)=\varnothing$, and $|\Sigma|\ge7$. For a nonzero rank-one matrix, its left Weyl translates span a 7-dimensional left ideal: their image vectors span the full 7-dimensional space. Select seven translates that form a basis. The support of each lies in a translate of $\Sigma$. All 49 labels occur in the union over all Weyl translates, so they must occur in the union of these seven basis supports. Hence $49\le7|\Sigma|$.

The complete support census in characteristic 29 has 544 masks in six types. Their support sizes are $7,14,14,20,21,21$, and their orbit sizes under the boundary symmetry group are respectively $48,48,48,336,48,16$. The lower bound is explicit: five representatives supported on unions of full Weyl rows have orbit sizes $48,48,48,48,16$, and a rank-one representative of size 20 has an orbit of size 336. The independent upper check imposes the necessary support conditions on every conjugate under the order-336 group. There are 343 base Boolean patterns and 2,401 patterns after group closure. The closed model is unsatisfiable after excluding the 544 realized masks. The unclosed model is satisfiable, so the conjugate closure is essential.

The same independent checker replays 25 polynomial unit identities: 21 expanded members and four arithmetic circuits. Each has multiplier-times-generator sum exactly 1. Ten rectangle-cover checks exclude all remaining positive rational weight cells for the five rectangular support types. The 20-element type is ruled out directly by its necessary feature-projection weight conditions, without a rectangle cover. The support table and these weight exclusions are distinct parts of the argument: supports alone do not rule out a trait approaching the boundary.

The identity replay authenticates every expanded member and every original generator used by a circuit. It matches the model equations and their weight initial forms, including character projections. A linear generator that does not match an individual basis row must lie in the span of the entries of $\sum_q z_qD_q$ outside that certificate's rectangle $A\times B$. Exact Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb F_{29}$ checks this span after the chart substitution, including constant terms. Each identity records `unmatched_linear_in_block_span` and `unmatched_not_explained`. Acceptance requires every unmatched member or used circuit leaf to be explained, with an empty unexplained list. The circuit check uses the complete dependency set rather than the historical diagnostic sample.

The ten rectangle blocks cover the whole residual rank-one components, not selected residue points. A block with rectangle $A\times B$ retains the complete parameter space $\mathbb P^{|A|-1}\times\mathbb P^{|B|-1}$. Its added linear relations have rank $|\Sigma|-|A||B|$, leaving every coordinate in the rectangle free. The catalogue is exhaustive over the nonempty coordinate sets, then grouped under translations. The eight non-square blocks use only their seven diagonal shears. Only the two self-reflective blocks—the $1\times1$ and the relevant $2\times2$—admit the full fourteen shear/reflection images. Filtering is performed before any weight cut. Applying fourteen images to a non-square block would add invalid coverage assumptions. The auxiliary relations constrain only the leading residue coordinates $z_q$, $q\in\Sigma$. The full trait retains every positive-weight correction, and no positive-weight trait coordinate is set to zero.

Four of the 25 identities are portable arithmetic-circuit certificates. Their frozen directed acyclic graphs are replayed from their source generators through each affine operation, row operation, and unit correction, and the final multiplier combination is recomputed exactly. Dependency tracking identifies every original polynomial used by a nonzero final multiplier. For each such active leaf, the certificate records all terms of minimum weight and proves every other term has strictly greater weight. Those equalities and strict inequalities hold throughout the entire open weight cell, so the same leaves and the same exact unit identity remain valid across the cell. The replayed hard circuit uses 28 affine steps and checks 35,432 local identities. The stored multiplier calculation is kept as a finite circuit rather than expanded.

The circuit's normalized identity is transferred back to the original ring without division by a weight denominator. First verify a true integral grading for every generator used in the frozen circuit, with the fixed leading coordinate of degree zero. If it has $G(t)=q>0$, $G(y)=-q$, and $G(z_v)\ge0$, set

$$
 A=\mathbb F[z,t,y]/(ty-1),\qquad B=A[u]/(u^q-t).
$$

Then $B$ is free over $A$ with basis $1,u,\ldots,u^{q-1}$, and $u$ is a unit. Substitute $z_v\mapsto z_vu^{-G(z_v)}$ in the normalized identity. Homogeneity expresses it in the original generators over $B$. Taking the $A$-linear coefficient of $u^0$ gives an identity equal to 1 over $A$. Replacing negative powers of $t$ by powers of $y$ and restoring $ty-1$ gives the original-ring unit identity. This uses no division by $q$, so remains valid when the residue characteristic divides $q$. The public proof interface is the fixed `independent/d7-finiteness/kill_nf5.py` verifier and the frozen chunk records named by `sources/d7-finiteness/numerics/e33_merge_p29.json`.

The integer grading need not be proportional to the valuation weights. Homogeneity and identification with the correct weight initial forms are separate checks.

### From initial-form exclusions to finiteness

Choose a prime of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)$ above 29 for which $\tau$ reduces to 16, and let $\mathcal O$ be its discrete valuation ring. Since $29\equiv1\pmod7$, the cyclotomic polynomial splits at 29. The 40,131 nonzero coefficients of the 441 quadratic rank-one minors were checked at all six roots. Every coefficient valuation is zero. The product and trace equations also have unit coefficients at 29. Thus the working residue-field initial forms are the reductions of the integral initial forms, with no term lost through divisibility by 29. The weight equations are homogeneous under positive rescaling, so every rational positive weight ray meets the normalized slice $\mu=1$. No denominator or weight cutoff is used. The argument includes traits with ramification, residue-field extension, and mixed characteristic valuation.

For a hypothetical trait from the open fibre $t\ne0$ to a boundary point $t=0$, valuations of the $z_p$ give a rational weight vector. A polynomial projection with at least two terms cannot have a unique lowest-weight term at a solution. The 25 unit identities and ten complete rectangle covers exclude every possible weight cell over the 544 supports. The 20-element type has no admissible weight even before cell subdivision. Hence there is no such trait.

Take the proper horizontal closure of the open model over $\mathcal O$. Its special fibre does not meet $t=0$, by the preceding trait test and the valuative criterion for properness ([Stacks Project, tag 0CM1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CM1)). The closure therefore lies in the affine open complement of the ample boundary divisor. A proper affine morphism is finite. Its generic fibre is the full open scheme $Y_7$, so $Y_7$ is finite over the characteristic-zero field. No length of the prime-29 open fibre is used.

### The parameter map, lifting, and disjointness

**Lemma (the actual face family).** Put $V=H^0(X_7,\mathcal O(2,2))$ and $\mathcal P=\operatorname{Mat}_{12\times24}(\mathbb C)\times V^{12}$. For every listed weight $w$, the map from $(M,\mathbf r)\in\mathcal P$ to the twelve direct-face rows is surjective onto $W_w^{12}$, where $W_w$ is the span of the global lowest parts of the $q_p$ and the allowed perturbation monomials. The certified simple-root count holds on a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $\mathcal P$.

To see this, let $u$ be the chart coordinates and $m_w$ the global minimum weight among all terms of $\mathbf q$ and $\varepsilon V$, with $v(\varepsilon)=1$. Let $q_{p,w}$ consist of the terms of $q_p$ of weight $m_w$, allowing $q_{p,w}=0$, and let $A_w$ contain the perturbation exponents with $1+\langle w,\alpha\rangle=m_w$. If $r_{i,\alpha}$ is the coefficient of $u^\alpha$ in row $r_i$, the map is

$$
 \rho_w(M,\mathbf r)_i
 =\sum_p M_{ip}q_{p,w}+\sum_{\alpha\in A_w}r_{i,\alpha}u^\alpha,
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,12.
$$

The coefficients of the displayed generators are independent within and between rows, so $\rho_w$ is a surjective linear map. This is exactly the global-minimum construction in `face_at` and the row construction in `generic_square` in [e10_face_system.py](../sources/d7/numerics/e10_face_system.py). The code uses the harmless common scaling $8q_p$.

Simple modular roots lift by Hensel's lemma at the recorded coefficient choices and persist as distinct simple characteristic-zero roots under small coefficient changes. For the listed weights, $m_w=1$ and the constant perturbation monomial belongs to $W_w$, so the general face system is finite on the torus. Its degree and simplicity are constant on a nonempty Zariski-open stratum, which meets the neighbourhood of the lifted roots. Thus a nonempty open subset of $W_w^{12}$ has at least the certified count. Its pull-back under the surjective map $\rho_w$ is nonempty and open in $\mathcal P$, proving the lemma.

Clearing weight denominators and substituting $\varepsilon=t^h$, $u_j=t^{hw_j}v_j$ turns each nonsingular leading root into a unique Puiseux branch by the formal implicit-function theorem. One root gives one branch. The conjugate branches under $\varepsilon^{1/h}\mapsto\xi\varepsilon^{1/h}$, with $\xi\in\mu_h$, correspond to the $\mu_h$-torus images of the leading root. These are distinct simple roots of the same quasi-homogeneous face system and are already included in its root count. Do not multiply by the weight denominator. Two primes are cross-checks and are not added.

The six strata are open coordinate-support strata. They are disjoint by their actual nonzero leading coordinates. Within a chart, distinct exact valuation vectors cannot describe the same branch, and different leading roots at the same valuation give different branches. Endpoints of a projective line are outside its counted open stratum and are compared after changing charts.

For separation across Weyl eigenbasis directions, each counted support contains at least one full affine line of seven Weyl labels and is contained in at most three parallel lines of its own direction. A line in a different direction meets those three lines in at most three labels, so it cannot be a full line in the same support. The $z$ family retains two full parallel lines even on its 20-label cancellation curve. That curve is already part of $z$ and is not counted again. This recovers the direction uniquely from actual Weyl support.

The counted face limits lie in the boundary base locus

$$
 J_7=V(\mathbf q)\cap\{S=0\}.
$$

Indeed, in a Weyl eigenbasis let $A=\{k:a_k\ne0\}$, $B=\{k:b_k\ne0\}$, and $T=B-A$. Every listed support has $0\notin T$ and $T\cap(-T)=\varnothing$. Thus $S=b^Ta=0$, and for each nonzero label $p=(i,j)$, either $i\notin T$ and $b^TD_pa=0$, or $-i\notin T$ and $b^TD_{-p}a=0$. Therefore every product $(b^TD_pa)(b^TD_{-p}a)$ vanishes and $\mathbf q=0$. These branch limits are in $J_7$, not in the open target $Y_7$.

There are eight directions, since $(7^2-1)/(7-1)=8$. In one direction the six open strata have respectively 42, 84, 84, 42, 28, and 84 distinct placements. Thus the orbit multipliers are

$$
 336=112\cdot3,\quad672=112\cdot6,\quad672=112\cdot6,
 \quad336=112\cdot3,\quad224=112\cdot2,\quad672=112\cdot6.
$$

Multiplying representative roots by these factors gives

$$
 C\ge112(3q+6l_1+6l_2+3z+2x_1+6x_2)
 =112(14920)=\boxed{1671040}.
$$

For each placement, the Clifford coordinate change preserves the span of $\mathbf q$ and the full perturbation space $V$, so it induces an invertible change of $(M,\mathbf r)$ in $\mathcal P$. Transporting the lemma's open sets therefore gives a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the same parameter space for every placement and weight. There are only finitely many such conditions. Their intersection is nonempty because $\mathcal P$ is irreducible, so one common general perturbation realizes all counted paths. A fixed generic matrix $M$ generally has no Clifford symmetry.

The 49-element Weyl–Heisenberg displacement group acts freely on these points. It therefore gives 672 displacement orbits. Keep this group distinct from the order-49 subgroup $\langle Z,S\rangle$ used in an older quotient-coordinate construction: that subgroup contains a nontrivial symplectic shear and is not the Weyl–Heisenberg kernel. The 98-element group $H_{98}$ used above is yet another group, namely displacements plus unitary parity. None is the full Clifford group or its transpose extension.

## 5. The Bézout budget forces equality and reducedness

Finiteness is needed here. Since $Y_7$ is finite, take a general $12\times24$ matrix $M$. Away from the base locus $V(\mathbf q)$, each point imposes 12 independent linear conditions on $M$, so the incidence variety has dimension equal to the parameter space and a general fibre is zero-dimensional. A positive-dimensional component of $M\mathbf q=0$ through a base point would either meet $X_7\setminus V(\mathbf q)$, contradicting that generic-fibre statement, or lie in $V(\mathbf q)\cap\{S\ne0\}=Y_7$, which is finite. Thus every point of $Y_7$ is locally isolated for general $M$, and

$$
 m_x:=\operatorname{length}\!\left(\mathcal O_{X_7,x}/(M\mathbf q)_x\right)\ge1.
$$

Each of the 12 equations in a general square subsystem $M\mathbf q=0$ has bidegree $(2,2)$. The multihomogeneous Bézout number on $\mathbb P^6\times\mathbb P^6$ is

$$
 B=2^{12}\binom{12}{6}=3,784,704.
$$

Perturb the square system by a general $12$-tuple $\mathbf r$ of bidegree-$(2,2)$ forms:

$$
 M\mathbf q+\varepsilon\mathbf r=0.
$$

The three certified path families can be required simultaneously. Each condition holds on a nonempty open subset of the irreducible full parameter space. Only finitely many conditions occur, so their intersection is nonempty. The special-fibre limits divide into three disjoint categories:

- The 21,232 free-orbit families have $\mathbf q\ne0$.
- The counted boundary branches lie in $J_7=V(\mathbf q)\cap\{S=0\}$.
- The target points lie in $Y_7=V(\mathbf q)\cap\{S\ne0\}$.

Thus off-base roots cannot coincide with either base-locus category, and the two base-locus categories are separated by $S$. Conservation of local intersection number gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
 3,784,704=B
 &\ge N_{\rm spur}+C+\sum_{x\in Y_7}
   \operatorname{length}\!\left(\mathcal O_{X_7,x}/(M\mathbf q)_x\right)\\
 &\ge 2,080,736+1,671,040+32,928\\
 &=3,784,704.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second inequality uses the certified 32,928-point lower bound and $m_x\ge1$. Equality holds throughout. Therefore $|Y_7|=32,928$, every $m_x=1$, and no additional solution exists.

At each solution, the local ring $\mathcal O_{X_7,x}$ is regular of dimension 12. A quotient of length one forces $(M\mathbf q)_x=\mathfrak m_x$. Because the rows of $M\mathbf q$ are linear combinations of the original equations,

$$
 (M\mathbf q)_x\subseteq(\mathbf q)_x\subseteq\mathfrak m_x,
$$

so both inclusions are equalities and $(\mathbf q)_x=\mathfrak m_x$. Thus the original scheme is reduced at every point, and its equation Jacobian has rank 12. Reducedness is deduced after the length squeeze. It is not assumed from a geometric-point count.
